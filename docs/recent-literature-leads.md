# Recent literature leads: material characteristics and tuning strategies

This note records search-result summaries supplied to the project on
2026-10-28. The summaries are useful for setting research questions, but the
full papers and bibliographic details have not yet been checked. They are not
training labels or verified claims. Several search snippets omit formulas,
transition temperatures, and pressures, so those values are intentionally not
reconstructed here.

## What the leads suggest to investigate

For conventional electron–phonon superconductors, the most relevant variables
are the electronic states at the Fermi level, electron–phonon coupling,
phonon frequencies, and whether the proposed crystal structure remains stable
at the required pressure and temperature. Hydrogen-rich compounds can combine
light atomic mass and high-frequency vibrations to raise the predicted or
measured transition temperature, but many headline examples require pressures
far beyond a practical metal casing. A high predicted (T_c) alone is therefore
not a useful target unless the structure is stable and the pressure is
feasible.

For nickelates and cuprates, the leads emphasize carrier doping, chemical
pressure from substitution, lattice distortion, phase purity, and competition
or coexistence with magnetism and charge order. These are coupled controls:
changing composition can alter both the electronic state and the crystal
structure. A study that observes a higher (T_c) under pressure does not by
itself establish that the same effect can be achieved at ambient pressure by
chemical substitution.

The practical tuning "knobs" mentioned are hydrostatic pressure, chemical
doping/substitution, strain, nanostructuring or interfaces, and light or
electrostatic carrier control. For this project, prioritize knobs that could
leave a stable, reproducibly synthesizable bulk material after the control is
removed. Pressure-dependent phases are valuable scientific evidence, but need
to be tagged with their pressure rather than merged with ambient-pressure
records.

## Implications for our pipeline

1. Add explicit descriptors for composition, crystal structure/phase, pressure,
   measurement method, and evidence type. Do not collapse calculated and
   measured (T_c) values into one label.
2. For electron–phonon candidates, capture density of states near the Fermi
   level, electron–phonon coupling, phonon stability, and formation/phase
   stability when source data provide them.
3. For unconventional families, preserve doping level, structural distortion,
   phase purity, and magnetic/charge-order observations as separate fields.
4. Evaluate pressure feasibility as a first-class target. Separate ambient,
   casing-achievable, and extreme-pressure results.
5. Verify every lead against the publisher or a stable preprint and capture a
   DOI, exact composition, measured/calculated status, (T_c), pressure, and
   method before using it in the evidence database.

The most directly relevant next reading is the 2023 Pickett review for the
theory/materials-design overview, followed by the 2024 nickelate paper for an
example where composition and pressure jointly change structure and
superconductivity. The hydride papers are useful mechanistic references, but
their pressure requirements may make them poor direct candidates for the
project's engineering target.
